    How these are worked out

    Positions come from the SGP4 orbit model run on the latest public two-line elements for the ISS. A pass counts as visible when the ISS is in sunlight while your sky is dark enough, and it is listed if it spends long enough above your chosen elevation while visible.

    Brightness is an estimate from distance and the Sun–ISS–observer angle, using a phase law fitted to standard ISS predictions, so expect it to be within about 0.3 magnitudes. Lower numbers are brighter: Sirius is −1.5, Jupiter about −2.5 and Venus about −4.

    Sky charts show the sky as you see it lying on your back with your head to the north: north at the top, east on the left, the zenith in the middle. The dashed ring marks your minimum elevation. The thick line is the part you can see; the dotted line is the ISS in Earth’s shadow or in daylight.
