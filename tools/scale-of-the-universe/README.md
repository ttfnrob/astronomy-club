# The Scale of the Universe

Zoom from the Planck length to the edge of the observable universe, with everything drawn to scale. Objects sit around a central focal point: as you zoom out, smaller things shrink into the middle and larger ones arrive from the edges. Tap any object for its size and a short note, or use **Browse** to search and jump to anything. It uses British spelling and mostly British and European examples, from a 1p coin and a red phone box to Ben Nevis, the Channel Tunnel and the Danube.

Inspired by Scale of the Universe by Cary and Michael Huang. The layout, drawings and descriptions here are new.

## How it works

- `index.html` is self-contained HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript with no libraries, build step, external fonts or network requests.
- Images are transparent WebP files in `images/`, loaded locally. There are no live data requests, analytics or user-data collection.
- Sizes are typical or approximate values. Each object is drawn at its stated size; for photos the stated dimension (length, height, diameter or wingspan, shown in the info panel) is matched to the photo, so perspective in some photos makes them approximate.
- Stars whose surfaces cannot be photographed in detail are shown with a recoloured photo of the Sun, and their info panels say so.

## Image credits

All photos come from Wikimedia Commons under the licences listed; each object's info panel links to its source and, where applicable, the licence. Everyday objects, animals and landmarks were cut out from their backgrounds and resized. Images are also converted to WebP for the site; these changes are disclosed in each info panel. Adapted CC BY-SA images are redistributed under the same or a compatible share-alike licence. Objects without a suitable free photo (for example UK coins, which are Crown copyright, atoms and lengths) are simple drawings.
